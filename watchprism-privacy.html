Privacy Policy

Effective date: November 13, 2026 · Last updated: November 13, 2026

This Privacy Policy explains what information the Watchprism mobile app ("Watchprism" or "the app") collects, why, who receives it, and the choices you have. "We", "our" and "us" mean its developer (see Section 1). It applies to Watchprism for iPhone (App Store) and for Android (Google Play).

Watchprism was previously called The Movie DB. On Google Play it is listed as "Movie Database: Movies & TV" (package uz.fozilbekimomov.the_movie_db) until that listing is renamed Watchprism. It is the same app, and this policy replaces the earlier version published at this address.

Watchprism is an independent app. It uses the TMDB API but is not endorsed or certified by TMDB (The Movie Database).

The short version

  • You can use Watchprism without an account. Signing in with a TMDB account is optional.
  • If you sign in, your TMDB username and password go directly to TMDB over an encrypted connection. We never receive or store your password.
  • Payments are handled by Apple or Google. We never see your card details.
  • We use Google Firebase for analytics, crash reports, push notifications and remote settings. We use Google AdMob for ads in the free version.
  • Our Google Analytics is linked to Google Ads, which we use to advertise Watchprism. Google may use analytics data, including purchases, to measure our ads and for ad personalisation. The app limits this with Google's consent mode: on iPhone these ad uses stay off unless you allow tracking when the app asks and, in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, also consent, and they always stay off with Premium. On Android (versions after 3.2.0) they follow your choices in Google's consent message in those regions and are not limited elsewhere (see Section 5.2). Usage statistics are collected either way.
  • On iPhone, your advertising identifier (IDFA) is used only if you allow tracking when the app asks.
  • Your history, follows, reminders and similar data are stored on your device; we do not upload them to our server. The app uses some of them in requests to TMDB, and some related actions are logged to Google Analytics (see Sections 5 and 6).
  • We do not sell your personal data for money. Some data is shared with Google for advertising, which US state laws may call "sharing" (see Section 16).

3. Information you provide

3.1 Signing in with TMDB (optional)

Watchprism does not have its own user accounts. You can skip sign-in ("Skip for now") and still browse, search, see where to watch, follow shows, set reminders and buy Premium.

If you choose to sign in, you type your TMDB username and password into the app. The app sends them over HTTPS directly to TMDB (The Movie Database, operated by Xperi Inc.) to create a TMDB session. The password is not stored on your device and is never sent to us.

After sign-in, the app keeps the following in its private storage on your device: the TMDB request token, the TMDB session ID and your TMDB account ID. It also loads your TMDB profile (for example your username and avatar) from TMDB to show it in the app. Of that profile, only the account ID is saved.

The app does not create TMDB accounts. On Android, the "Register on TMDB" link on the sign-in screen opens TMDB's website in your browser; the iPhone app has no such link. Your use of TMDB is governed by TMDB's privacy policy.

3.2 What you save to your TMDB account

When you are signed in, these actions are sent to TMDB and stored in your TMDB account, on TMDB's servers:

  • adding or removing favourites and watchlist items;
  • rating movies and TV shows, or removing a rating;
  • creating, editing or deleting custom lists (name, description, items).

According to TMDB, your watchlist, favourites and rated lists are private by default, and you can choose to make them public on TMDB's website (TMDB account FAQ).

Custom lists are different. TMDB's developer documentation names private lists as a feature of its newer (version 4) list interface. Watchprism creates lists through TMDB's version 3 list interface, which has no privacy setting (TMDB: v3 or v4 lists). Treat a list you create in the app as public: other people may be able to see its name, description and titles on themoviedb.org, and the app's "Share" button shares a link to it. Do not put personal information in a list's name or description. You can delete a list in the app or on themoviedb.org.

3.3 Searches and browsing

To show results, the app sends what you type in Search to TMDB, together with the app's TMDB API key, your app language and, for streaming availability, a country code (your "watch region"). The watch region is the country you pick in "My services", or else the country of your device's region setting, or else a country based on your app language. TMDB also receives your IP address, as with any internet request. The app keeps your last 10 searches on your device; Google Analytics only receives the length of a search, not its text.

If you are signed in, opening a title also asks TMDB, using your TMDB session, whether that title is in your favourites, watchlist or ratings, so TMDB can associate the titles you open with your TMDB account. For You also loads your TMDB ratings and watchlist to choose recommendations.

To provide some features, the app also sends TMDB the IDs of items stored on your device: the shows you follow (to refresh episode data), recently viewed movies and the genres you picked during onboarding (to build For You), and your selected streaming services (for the "Only on my services" filter). See Section 6.

3.4 When you email us

If you contact us from the app or by email, we receive your email address and whatever you write. This includes a report you start with "Report to us" in a TMDB review's menu: your mail app opens a message to us that already names the review, the title it belongs to and a link to the review. We use your message only to answer you and, for a review report, to act on it.

4. Purchases (Premium)

Watchprism Premium is sold as a monthly or annual auto-renewing subscription or as a one-time lifetime purchase. Purchases are processed entirely by Apple (App Store) on iPhone or Google Play on Android. We never receive your name, card number, billing address or Apple ID / Google account details.

What the app receives and does:

  • From the store, the app learns which Premium product you own and the status of a purchase (purchased, pending approval such as Ask to Buy, cancelled, failed, restored, refunded or revoked).
  • On iPhone the app reads your App Store transactions through Apple's StoreKit 2 on your device. It asks the App Store for a full re-sync, which may ask you to sign in to your Apple Account, only when you tap Restore Purchase.
  • The app stores on your device whether Premium is active and which plan you own.
  • Receipts and transactions are not sent to our server or any other server we run. We do not have a purchase backend.
  • Google Analytics receives Premium funnel events (for example: paywall opened, purchase button tapped, free trial started (with the plan), purchase completed, cancelled, pending or failed with the store's error message, restore completed, "Manage subscription" tapped (with your plan)). It also receives a "premium: yes/no" property. Separately, Google Analytics automatically records in-app purchase events from the App Store and Google Play, which include the product, its price and currency.
  • If you allow notifications, your device joins a "premium users" or "free users" notification topic so that announcements can be aimed at either group.

Billing, renewals, cancellations and refunds are governed by Apple's or Google's terms. See Section 13 and our support page for how to manage or cancel a subscription.

On iPhone, Analytics also collects the IDFV, and the IDFA only if you allowed tracking. On Android, it collects the Android advertising ID as described in 5.1.

The app turns them on only if you tap "Allow" on Apple's tracking prompt and, in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, you also consent in Google's consent message (see 5.6); there each one follows the purposes you accepted. Premium does not change this. Elsewhere, and until the consent message has reported your status on your device (for example just after you install the app), the app does not limit them. 5.3 Crash reports (Firebase Crashlytics)

If the app crashes or hits certain errors (for example a failed start-up step or an image that does not load), Crashlytics sends a report with the stack trace, the state of the app at the time, the device model, the operating system version and the Crashlytics and Firebase installation IDs. We do not add your name, account or custom logs to crash reports.

5.4 Push notifications (Firebase Cloud Messaging)

When the app starts, Firebase Cloud Messaging creates a push token for this installation (on iPhone it is linked to Apple's push (APNs) token), whether or not you allow notifications. The token stays with Firebase; the app does not send it to our server. The app asks for permission before showing notifications. Notifications are shown only if you allow them, and only then does your device join these notification topics:

  • "all users";
  • "new trailers" and "trending", which you can turn off in Settings → Notifications;
  • one topic for your app language;
  • "premium users" or "free users".

Topics reveal only that group membership, not who you are. To subscribe the device to topics, Firebase receives the device model, language, time zone, operating system, app identifier and app version. Notifications about the shows you follow and about release reminders are scheduled locally on your device. The list of shows you follow and your reminders are stored on your device. To keep new-episode alerts up to date, the app asks TMDB for the details of each followed show (by its TMDB ID). Following or unfollowing a show and setting a reminder are also logged to Google Analytics with the title's TMDB ID (see 5.2).

5.5 Remote settings (Firebase Remote Config and Installations)

On start, the app downloads settings from Firebase Remote Config. These include feature switches, update requirements, the "What's new" text and the "Our apps" list. To choose settings, Firebase receives the country code, language, time zone, OS version and app identifiers. It also uses the Firebase installation ID. The app sends no other data of its own to Remote Config.

5.6 Advertising (Google AdMob)

The free version shows ads from Google AdMob: an ad when the app opens, banners, full-screen ads between some screens and optional rewarded ads (for example, "watch a short ad to go ad-free for 20 minutes"). Android also shows native ads in some lists. Premium users see no ads. For them the app does not show the tracking prompt or the consent form and does not start the ads SDK.

According to Google, the Google Mobile Ads SDK may collect:

  • crash logs and performance data;
  • a device ID (the advertising identifier or other app- or developer-scoped identifiers);
  • advertising data (ads you have seen);
  • coarse location, including location derived from your IP address;
  • product interactions such as taps.

Google uses this data under its own policies.

Your ad choices on a device without Premium:

  1. iPhone: the app shows Apple's App Tracking Transparency prompt once, the first time it is in the foreground after you install it, normally before the first ad loads. If the app starts in the background (for example from a notification), ads may load before the prompt, which then appears when you next open the app. Until you answer, and if you do not allow tracking, the app requests non-personalised ads and the IDFA is not available. If you allow tracking, AdMob may use your IDFA to personalise and measure ads. Your answer also decides whether the ad uses of analytics data are on (see 5.2).
  2. Where the law requires consent (for example in the EEA and the UK), Google's consent message (Google User Messaging Platform, UMP) asks for your choices about ads. In some US states, Google's message may instead let you opt out of the use of your data for targeted advertising. You can change your choices later in Settings → About → Privacy options. This item appears when it applies to you. In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, these choices also apply to the ad uses of analytics data (see 5.2; on Android from the versions after 3.2.0).

Android only: some ads may be served by Meta Audience Network through AdMob mediation. According to Meta, its SDK may process device identifiers, device attributes (such as device model, screen size, operating system and SDK versions) and ad performance information (such as impressions, clicks, placement and viewability). The iPhone app does not include Meta's SDK.

The iPhone app lists Apple SKAdNetwork IDs for ad networks. These let ad networks measure app installs that come from ads without using the IDFA.

5.7 Our install counter

Every time the app starts, it sends one small message over HTTPS to our own server. The server runs on Railway (a hosting provider) and stores its records in Google Cloud Firestore, in a United States multi-region location. The message contains:

  • a random install ID that the app creates and keeps on your device;
  • on iPhone, the Identifier for Vendor (IDFV); on Android, the Android OS build ID (a build label shared by all devices running the same Android build, not a device identifier);
  • the app version, the platform (iOS or Android) and the time.

The server keeps one record per installation: the install ID, the IDFV (iPhone) or OS build ID (Android), the platform, when the installation was first seen, the first and the latest app version, and when the latest version was first opened. It also keeps one record of the first open of each app version on that installation, with the same identifiers, the version, the time and whether it was a new install, an update or a reinstall. Later starts of the same version are not stored. To tell a reinstall from a new install, the server compares the IDFV (iPhone) or OS build ID (Android) with its earlier records.

The server does not store your IP address in these records and does not work out your location from it. Like any web server, it sees your IP address when the message arrives, and its request log writes that IP address to the hosting provider's (Railway's) request logs, which are kept under Railway's log retention. We use this data only to count installs, updates and reinstalls of the app. It is not linked to your name, email or TMDB account, and it is not used for ads.

6. Data that stays on your device

The following is stored in the app's private storage on your phone. The app does not upload these lists to us. Some of it is used to request content from TMDB, as explained below.

  • Recently viewed titles (the last 20), "continue watching" progress and your last 10 searches.
  • A viewing log for Stats and Year in Movies: titles opened, episodes marked watched and your ratings, with dates. It covers the current year and the two previous years.
  • Day streaks and badges.
  • Shows you follow (with their next-episode data), release reminders and the notifications scheduled for them.
  • Favourite people. These are local, not part of your TMDB favourites.
  • The authors of TMDB reviews you chose to hide ("Hide reviews from …").
  • Your streaming services ("My services"), saved filter sets, genres picked during onboarding, and your app settings: language, theme, country and notification preferences.
  • Premium status and plan, ad-free and unlock timers, and the install ID used by our install counter.
  • Your TMDB session (see 3.1).
  • Cached images and fonts. Share cards are created on your device and are only shared when you choose to.

To provide features, the app sends TMDB the IDs of some of these items: followed shows (to refresh episode data), recently viewed movies and onboarding genres (to build For You), and your selected streaming services (for the "Only on my services" filter). Some related events are sent to Google Analytics as described in 5.2 (for example, that you opened a title, followed a show or set a reminder, with the title's TMDB ID). Like other app data, this data may be included in your device backups (iCloud or Google) if you have backups turned on.

12. How long data is kept

  • On your device: until you clear it in the app (where the app offers that, see Section 14), sign out (TMDB session only) or delete the app.
  • Crashlytics: Google keeps crash stack traces and the related identifiers for 90 days and then starts removing them.
  • Firebase installation IDs (used by Cloud Messaging and Remote Config): Google keeps them until they are deleted. Google may delete an installation's ID after 270 days without activity. Once an ID is deleted, Google says the data tied to it is removed from live and backup systems within 180 days.
  • Google Analytics: user-level and event-level data is kept for up to 14 months; this does not affect aggregated reports.
  • Ads: Google and Meta keep data under their own retention policies.
  • TMDB account data: kept by TMDB until you delete it or your TMDB account.
  • Our install counter: the records described in 5.7 are not deleted automatically. They are kept while the install counter runs, and we delete them on request (see Section 14). IP addresses in the hosting provider's request logs are kept under Railway's log retention.
  • Emails to us: as long as needed to answer you and keep a record of the conversation.

13. Your choices: ads, tracking, notifications

Ads and tracking

  • Premium removes all ads, and with them the tracking prompt, the consent form and the ads SDK. It does not turn off Google Analytics measurement. On iPhone it also keeps the ad uses of analytics data off (5.2); on Android it does not change them.
